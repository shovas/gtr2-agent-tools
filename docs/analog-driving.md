## Analog driving via vJoy, lap tuning and garage exit (2026-11-11)

**Setup.** vJoy 2.1.8 (kernel driver, installed once with admin; the user approved it) gives a virtual joystick that `scripts/gtr2_driving/vjoy.py` drives from unelevated Python (`VJoy(1).axis("Z", 0..1)`; every axis is centred on acquire and release, because ResetVJD leaves sliders at 0 and the game reads that as input). The game lists it as JOY1 (Controls > Buttons); its steering/throttle/brake bars follow the axes as |value-0.5|*2, each direction a half range. Profile codes `(1, N)`: X+=1, X-=2, Y+=3, Y-=4, Z+=5, Z-=6, Rx+=7, Rx-=8. Committed bindings: steering X, Accelerate `(1, 6)` = Z below centre, Brake `(1, 8)` = Rx below centre (both at once works), shifts on vJoy buttons 1/2, `Basic Controls="0"`. To rebind: click the row (twice, the first only hovers) and move that axis while the "PRESS KEY OR CONTROLLER BUTTON" box is up. First gear still uses keyboard Q (clutch), so the game needs the focus for that one step.

**Using it.** `gtr2-lap-check.py LAYOUT --input analog [--quick] [--runs N] [--drive-args "..."]` (`--quick` stops at the first start/finish crossing, the out lap from the pits, about 3 min); `gtr2-drive-lap.py --aiw ... --fast --input analog [--tuned] [--zones] [--trace N]`. The lap runner always sets the Murcielago and writes `gtr2_ui/runs/lap_drive.log`. The control loop is plain scripted code (pure pursuit on the AIW line, speed targets from the bends ahead, proportional throttle/brake, rpm shifts); no model runs while driving. Between the commands and the axes a 100 Hz writer runs a `RackModel` per axis (damped spring on an inertia with sliding and breakaway friction, speed-capped): steering inertia 1.0, friction 1.0, stiction 1.5, wn 14; throttle `0.7 0.5 0.8 20`, brake `1.2 1.0 1.5 18` (`--analog-inertia/-friction/-stiction/-wn`, `--analog-throttle-model`, `--analog-brake-model`; inertia 0 = the older acceleration-limited `Follower`). The user asked for this so inputs look human.

**Mode and aids.** The committed `<profile>.PLR` is Novice (`Difficulty Type="0"`, auto shift, spin recovery, opposite lock and auto reverse on). The lap runner's driving profile (`DRIVING_PROFILE`, reverted with git afterwards) sets `Difficulty Type="2"` (the main menu header then reads **MODE: SIMULATION**, checked 2026-11-11), manual shift, spin recovery, opposite lock and auto reverse off, speed-sensitive steering 0.6. Steering Help 1, Brake Help 1, Stability Control 1, Auto Clutch 1, Invulnerability 1 and Damage Multiplier 100 stay on, so times are not comparable with a game without the aids. Every `lap_results.jsonl` row has a `profile` object (`MODE_KEYS` in `gtr2-lap-check.py`); rows before 2026-11-11 evening did not record it.

### Focus loss pauses the sim completely; a non-activating window does not (measured 2026-11-12)

`gtr2-drive-lap.py --focus-test START SECONDS` opens Notepad in front of the game mid-lap and reports the sim rate (distance moved / (speed x wall time)) before, during and after. **A window that takes focus stops the sim dead (rate 0.00, both with `Inactive Sleep Time` 25 and -1, so that key is irrelevant); it resumes at 1.00 when the window closes, and the lap still completes.** The same Notepad started minimised without activation (`STARTUPINFO.wShowWindow = SW_SHOWMINNOACTIVE`, env `GTR2_FT_NOACTIVATE=1`) left the rate at 1.00. So: anything the tools launch during a test must start hidden/minimised-no-activate (the nav server already runs under pythonw), and a manual window in front of the game costs sim time but not correctness. No profile or config switch for this was found.

### Driving needs vJoy (note for users of this repo)

Analog driving (`gtr2-drive-lap.py --input analog`, `gtr2-lap-check.py --input analog`, the tuner) feeds a virtual joystick. A new machine needs, once: (1) **vJoy** installed (2.1.8 used here; kernel driver, needs admin) with device 1 configured (X, Y, Z, Rx axes and at least 2 buttons); (2) the game's profile bound to it: in Controls > Buttons the game lists it as JOY1; steering X, Accelerate `(1, 6)` (Z below centre), Brake `(1, 8)` (Rx below centre), shifts on vJoy buttons 1 and 2, `Basic Controls="0"` in `UserData\<profile>\<profile>.PLR`. Rebind by clicking the row (twice, the first only hovers) and moving the axis while the "PRESS KEY OR CONTROLLER BUTTON" box is up. The game folder's tracked profile carries these bindings; a profile created on another machine does not. Keyboard driving needs no vJoy but cannot leave the garage box cleanly.

## A generic time-optimal racing line (`gtr2_driving/racingline.py`, `--line-opt`) and why it did not pay off yet (2026-11-16)

8. Gear choice for the exit (cargears) in live mode, and one gear lower for traction in slow corners.

### To do: gear choice in slow corners (user tip, 2026-11-18)

Real drivers seldom use 1st gear in a corner; every shift costs time and the car then has less rear traction on the exit. The live gear logic (`cargears.best_gear`, `--gear-opt`, the default in live mode) picks the gear with the most wheel thrust (torque times ratio) at the rpm the car would have in each gear at the current speed, rev limit permitting (hysteresis 1.04): that is always the lowest gear that does not over-rev, so below about 17 m/s it chooses 1st (the Imola logs show gear 1 at 15 to 17 m/s in the chicanes and a downshift on the way in), whatever it costs in shifts. Planned change: choose by lap time, not thrust: a downshift must pay for itself (a larger hysteresis for downshifts, no 1st gear above a minimum speed of about 8 m/s unless the car would otherwise fall under its idle revs, look at the next 1.5 s of the plan to see whether a shift back up follows right away); measure laps with the old and the new rule. Fixed numbers here are starting priors only: the cost of a shift and the real thrust at the exit should come from the car's own data and from the laps.

## Signal-driven driving: what the maps and the follower are, and how to make them respond like a driver (strategy, 2026-11-18)

**What exists now.** *Grip map*: one factor per 20 m of lap distance that multiplies the planned cornering speed (priors: 10 m/s^2 lateral). It rises 10% when the car used at least 85% of the planned lateral load cleanly, falls 2% on a slide (tyre grip fraction under 0.5), 8% on six bins after a track-limit event, and heals 4% per clean lap. *Braking map*: the deceleration the car has shown it can do, one number per 10 m/s speed band (prior 12 m/s^2): probed up 8% when clean, down 5% when the front slips over 2.5 times the target (lock), down 12% after a hit under braking, healing 3% per lap. *Steering follower*: pure pursuit: aim at a point 5 m + 1.2 s of speed ahead on the line, steering = heading error / 25 degrees - yaw rate / 90, with fixed gains (and a hand-speed limit); the pedals are a proportional law on speed error (gain 0.45, deadband 0.3 m/s). The maps learn a *number* from outcomes lap by lap; the follower and pedal laws learn nothing.

**The ideal.** Respond to the cues a driver feels, in the moment, with a small bounded correction, and keep what worked for next lap. Signals the driver already has: per-wheel grip fraction, wheel speed (slip), load, lateral/longitudinal/vertical acceleration, yaw rate, velocity direction against heading (sideslip), steering command, surface, tyre temperature and wear.

**Steering, from cues**
1. Understeer / scrub: measured yaw rate against what the identified response predicts for the steering given (`--steer-id`), plus a low front grip fraction while steering is large. Response: stop adding lock (clamp steering at the angle the front tyres have shown they can use at this speed: the measured peak of lateral force against steering), ease throttle or brake, and let the car run the wider arc.
2. Oversteer / spin: sideslip angle (velocity direction against heading) and rear grip fraction. Response: steer towards the velocity vector (counter-steer proportional to the slip angle and its rate), ease throttle.
3. Pursuit in velocity terms: aim the velocity vector, not the heading, at the look-ahead point, so a slide is corrected by the same law.
4. Gains from the car: identify gain and lag online (recursive least squares on yaw rate against command history, only with enough excitation), set the loop gain by pole placement for a chosen closed-loop time constant; fall back to the fixed gains when the estimate is not confident or a divergence monitor trips (the earlier `--st-learn` collapsed because its estimate fed itself). The static scaling tried today (`--steer-live`) ignored the lag.
5. Look-ahead from error: hill-climb the look-ahead time lap by lap against the lateral-error cost (like the session push), per section.

**Braking, from cues**
1. Brake points like a driver's markers: for each braking zone record the entry-speed error at the corner (arrived 3 m/s over: brake earlier by that distance next lap; arrived under with no lock: later). Harder and shorter without lock is the goal, so the cost is stopping distance at no lock.
2. Peak-seeking pedal: raise the pedal until deceleration stops rising or slip passes the tyre's peak (measure the decel-against-slip curve per speed band and hold the slip at its top); the braking map becomes a result, not a probe.
3. Trail braking: release the brake as lateral load rises (friction circle: brake share about sqrt(1 - (ay/ay_max)^2)).
4. Rear slip and traction on exit: the `--dyn` wheel-slip control already does the simple version; seek the throttle that holds drive-wheel slip at its best value.

**Grip map, from cues.** Replace probing with an estimate of the tyre limit from the grip fractions against load (downforce is fitted already), share information between neighbouring bins, keep a confidence per bin, track tyre temperature and wear as slow drifts.

**Experiments for open windows** (each opt-in, judged by a low-noise metric, not lap time alone; bisect any scalar; two runs; results recorded here):
- E1 Instrument only: log sideslip, yaw ratio, front and rear grip fractions over 10 laps; check whether they lead track-limit events and spins by a useful time. No control change.
- E2 Steering clamp at the measured useful angle (Spa Bus Stop swings; metric lateral rms and largest offset on the out lap).
- E3 Velocity-vector pursuit against heading pursuit (same metric).
- E4 Online gain-and-lag identification with pole placement and a divergence monitor (Spa, Imola, Monza out laps).
- E5 Per-corner brake-point learner (metric: entry-speed error, then lap time over a 10-lap session).
- E6 Peak-seeking pedal on a straight with repeated stops from 60 m/s (metric: stopping distance, no lock).
- E7 Trail braking by friction circle.
- E8 Grip map with confidence and neighbour sharing (metric: laps to the first clean fast lap).
Safeguards for all: bounded corrections, a fallback to the fixed prior when confidence is low, a divergence monitor, and the garage/pit/merge paths keep the fixed laws.

## Gravel-trap escape (2026-11-18)

Cause found in a Monza 4 live session (lap 4: 169.8 s, 40 s lost): the car arrived at the first chicane wide of the line, ran through into the gravel (30 m from the line) and crept out at 0 to 2.5 m/s for 37 s: the wheel-slip control (`--dyn`) cuts the throttle to 25% of its value whenever the driven wheels spin, which in gravel is always, and the stall recovery reversed again and again. User tip: in a trap use 1st gear and a steady, high but not maximum engine speed; some wheel spin is useful.

Test harness `--trap-test DIST` (Monza 4, DIST 1230, `--quick`): slow to 18 m/s, steer left off the road until stopped (all four tyres off the road, under 2 m/s for 1 s: 12.6 m from the line), then drive normally and time the escape to the first tyre on the road. Longer patience before reversing (`--trap-patience`) is untested: 1.0. Next: a wheel-spin ceiling (some spin, not maximum), other traps and gravel types, other cars.

Also added (live mode, untested): `--lat-guard` (target speed falls as the sideways error beyond 2.5 m grows: floor 0.5, span 8 m) and `--limit-brake-pen 0.10` (a track-limit event within 4 s of hard braking lowers the braking numbers of the speed bands between the limit speed and the braking start speed by 10%).

## Track limits by geometry only (2026-11-20, user rule: never by road surface)

Rule: a track-limit event is all four tyres wholly beyond the same boundary line. The surface rule (no tyre on a ROAD or DRAIN surface) is gone as a judge: it counted four wheels on a kerb as off the track and let the lap-time work chase false events. `--limit-rule` was removed; `--geo-limits` is kept as a no-op for old command lines. Surface names are still logged (`geo` in the result: `surface_only`, margins) as a diagnostic.

How it works (`gtr2_driving/limits.py`, offline checks in `scripts/test_limits.py`):
- **Boundary lines** are polylines through waypoint position +- road width along the waypoint's own perpendicular, built from the file's waypoints BEFORE line smoothing or the racing line move them (`Boundaries`). The old test measured from the nearest (moved) waypoint's cross line and subtracted the line's shift from the width; on 3667 logged Imola samples it differed from the boundary test by 1 cm at the median but more than 0.3 m on 144 samples (4%), p99 0.62 m, worst 2.7 m, all in corners, and it flipped the sign near the line (0.14 m beyond against 0.30 m inside at lap distance 2078 m).
- **Distance** is point to segment (the nearest of 13 boundary segments around the car's waypoint), signed by the side of the directed segment. A boundary built from 5 m waypoint spacing has its own chord error (s^2/8r, about 0.15 m at 12 m radius); the windowed search equals a brute-force distance to every segment (tested to 1e-9 m).
- **Tyres** are four footprint rectangles (width x 0.2 m patch) placed from the car's real wheel positions: the `WHEEL_xx_A.GMT` meshes of the car's `*WHEELS.GTR` give, for the Maserati MC12, front track 1.645 m, rear 1.650 m, wheelbase 2.80 m (1.40 m each side of the origin), tyre widths 0.306 m front and 0.380 m rear (the old fixed numbers were 1.65 m, 2.70 m and 0.30 m). A tyre is beyond a line only when all four footprint corners are. Fixed numbers (`--wheel-track`, `--tyre-half`) are used only if the files cannot be read, and the driver prints which it used.
- **Shared memory** has no tyre positions (rFactor 1 TelemInfoV2: car position, orientation matrix, per-tyre rotation, deflection, ride height, load, lateral force, grip fraction, temperatures, wear, terrain name), so the car's own meshes are the source.
- **Not yet verified:** that the telemetry position is the mesh origin (the car's centre of gravity is 57% on the rear, about 0.2 m behind mid-axle, if the telemetry position is the physics origin); steering angle and suspension travel are ignored (centimetres); and how the waypoint widths relate to the painted white line (earlier measurement: they reach 0.5 to 1 m past the road surface over the kerbs at Imola). `--limit-trim` pulls the lines in to calibrate.

First result with the geometric rule (Imola 4, Maserati, push fixed at 1.22, slow zones at 500, 1100 and 2500 m, fresh memory, out lap and 3 timed laps): 120.06 s clean, 120.80 s clean, 120.74 s with one impact; zero track-limit events (the surface rule gave 1 of 3 clean at the same push, best 122.80 s). 40 samples were surface-off but not geometric-off, none the other way round. The lap times fell by 3 to 4 s against the same zones at 1.17 under the surface rule because the live responses to false events (grip factors lowered, braking moved earlier) stopped firing.

End-to-end check of the committed code (driver reading the tyre geometry from the car's wheel meshes: track 1.645 / 1.650 m, tyre widths 0.31 / 0.38 m; same setting, push 1.22 with the three slow zones, 3 timed laps): 119.62 s, 120.74 s and 120.75 s, all clean, zero track-limit events (37 samples surface-off but not geometric-off, none the other way round).
